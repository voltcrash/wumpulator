    How it works

    Every safe cell is a vertex. Two safe cells that share a side are joined by an edge, so each legal move is one edge and every move costs the same. Pits and the Wumpus are not vertices at all.

    Each vertex stores an adjacency list of its neighbours in the fixed order up, right, down, left. Both searches read these lists in that order, so every run is reproducible.

    The small numbers on the board show the order in which cells were discovered. A search usually discovers many cells that never make it onto the final route.

    The route is rebuilt at the end: BFS follows parent links back from the gold, and DFS reads its stack from bottom to top. The faint lines on the board are those parent links.

    Time: O(V + E). A vertex is discovered at most once, so it enters the queue or stack at most once. Each adjacency list is scanned once, and together the lists hold 2E entries.

    Space: O(V). The discovered and parent arrays have one entry per vertex, and the queue or stack never holds more than V cells.

    These are operation counts, not seconds. The playback speed does not change them.
